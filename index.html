How keepers work

Keep up to two players from your last year's end-of-season roster.

Your keeper costs a pick from our upcoming draft.

Keeping a player means giving up a pick this year.

The keeper price is where the player was drafted last year.

Some nuances:

  • Players picked up on waivers who were undrafted cost a 15th-round pick.
  • Players picked up on waivers who had been drafted cost the round they were originally drafted in.
  • Two keepers can't cost the same round. If both would be a 15th, the second one moves up to a 14th.

The price goes up one round every year you keep the same player.

Your first year keeping a player, the player costs exactly the round they were drafted in. Each year after that, the cost moves up one round: a 10th becomes a 9th, then an 8th.

Note that the round number gets smaller as the price gets bigger. Giving up a 9th costs you more than giving up a 10th.

Once the price would pass round 1, the player can no longer be kept and re-enters the draft.

New this year: an IR slot

Each team gets one IR spot, so a player who lands on NFL injured reserve can be stashed instead of dropped. It only accepts a true IR or PUP designation — a week-to-week Out or Doubtful tag won't fit. This allows you to keep an injured player for next year, if you like.

How we'll run it this year

Starting next year the Sleeper app will handle the keeper picks (we'll still have to set their draft value by hand).

  1. Find your team below and pick your keeper(s), using the Costs to keep column.
  2. Post the player name and round in the league chat before the deadline.
  3. That round comes off your draft board. When your pick in that round comes up, it will be already spent on that player.
  4. Everything else drafts normally in the remaining rounds.

Deadline: not set yet — it needs to land before the draft. Sleeper's keeper_deadline is currently 0.
