New this year: an Injured Reserve (IR) slot

Each team gets one IR spot, so a player who lands on NFL injured reserve can be stashed instead of dropped. It only accepts a true IR or PUP designation — a week-to-week Out or Doubtful tag won't fit. This allows you to keep an injured player for next year, if you like.

Keeper Rules and Rosters

2026 Fantasy!

How keepers work

Keep one player from your last year's end-of-season roster.

We're starting with one keeper this year to learn the format. Next year we plan on two or three.

Your keeper costs a pick from our upcoming draft.

Keeping a player means giving up a pick this year.

The keeper price is where the player was drafted last year.

Some nuances:

  • Players picked up on waivers who were undrafted cost a 15th-round pick.
  • Players picked up on waivers who had been drafted cost the round they were originally drafted in.
  • Two keepers can't cost the same round. Not an issue this year, but once we go to two or three: if both would be a 15th, the second one moves up to a 14th.

The price goes up one round every year you keep the same player.

  • This is known as “The Escalator.”
  • Your first year keeping a player, the player costs exactly the round they were drafted in. Each year

after that, the cost moves up one round: a 10th becomes a 9th, then an 8th, and so on.

  • Once the price would pass round 1, the player can no longer be kept and re-enters the draft.

  1. Find your team from last year below and pick your keeper. The cost to keep each player is labelled (R1, R10, etc).
  2. Post your keeper choice in the Sleeper app league chat (or email Ben) before the deadline.
  3. If you don’t want a keeper or you miss the deadline, no big deal! You’ll get all your picks in the draft.
